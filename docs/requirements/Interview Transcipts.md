**Interview Questions**

Ten SIT students were interviewed using the following questions:

**Question 1:** As a respondent, through which channels do you typically receive survey or research participation requests?

**Question 2:** Have you ever intended to participate in a survey or research interview but were unable to do so? What was the reason?

**Question 3:** As a researcher, what was the most difficult part of gathering sufficient respondents before your deadline?

**Question 4:** What are your thoughts on our proposed system? What features or functionalities would you like to see included to improve your experience?

**Summary of Findings**

| Finding | Resulting requirement or feature |
| :---- | :---- |
| Research requests mostly arrive by email, where they get buried or skipped. | Centralized list of published studies. |
| Researchers find it hardest to reach respondents who meet all their qualifications. | Eligibility-based filtering. |
| Respondents want to see only the studies that match their qualifications. | Program and year level collected at first sign-in; filtering by eligibility. |
| Researchers want to see how many respondents have answered and how many more are needed. | Response counts and automatic statistics; response counts on the Your Researches and Research Summary screens. |
| Respondent motivation, incentives, and management or review of research were suggested. | Not included in the current scope; recorded for future consideration. |
| Scheduling interviews can be difficult when researchers and respondents have conflicting availability. | Interview scheduling feature that allows researchers to create and manage schedules and respondents to view and choose availability schedules |

**Interview Transcripts**

**Respondent 1**

**Q1:** Face-to-face invitations, or invitations sent through emails or QR codes.

**Q2:** None, but I did ignore some research requests that were not directed to me specifically but to the majority, like the ones sent by email.

**Q3:** Respondents’ willingness to answer, and asking permission from faculty.

**Q4:** A good initiative to have a centralized search for respondents, as it is prominent to find respondents in all schools, not just in SIT.

**Respondent 2**

**Q1:** Official department or faculty announcement emails, Facebook messages, or direct emails.

**Q2:** Survey requests sent through email that got buried under other inbox messages.

**Q3:** Permission from department representatives to request email broadcasts.

**Q4:** A time-to-complete badge and a filter to show surveys that match my qualifications.

**Respondent 3**

**Q1:** Email.

**Q2:** None. I try to always answer surveys if I see one in my email, unless they are buried under other emails.

**Q3:** Availability of the respondents.

**Q4:** The system is good. The purpose is there, but without a motive for respondents, they will most likely be too lazy to answer. I suggest that before one can upload their research, they must be a respondent to at least five other research studies.

**Respondent 4**

**Q1:** Messenger, usually; sometimes friends refer others or their own research. Gmail for school surveys. There were also times back then when teachers would upload research of other students and ask us to participate.

**Q2:** None. I’ve been a respondent for qualitative research before, and the researchers are the ones who adjust their time to interview me when I’m free.

**Q3:** Finding relevant and qualified respondents, especially for qualitative research.

**Q4:** Maybe adding filters where they can add all their qualities, details, hobbies, etc.

**Respondent 5**

**Q1:** Either from Google Forms or from the researchers themselves, who give us physical forms.

**Q2:** Yes, many times. Mostly it conflicts with my class schedule.

**Q3:** Finding qualified respondents who then lack the initiative to cooperate.

**Q4:** I suggest you check out the systems of survey apps that give cash incentives and see what features you can copy from them.

**Respondent 6**

**Q1:** Most commonly from email; other times the researchers share them directly.

**Q2:** Yes, mostly on survey forms sent to our email, as they are buried under all the other emails we receive. Sometimes I just straight up forgot to answer because I prioritized my schoolwork. There was also a situation regarding an interview, where the researchers had difficulty allocating a time that worked for both sides.

**Q3:** Not finding enough people who meet all of our qualifications. There are also instances where a respondent backs out and we can’t do anything about it because of research ethics.

**Q4:** I would like to see real-time notifications for scheduled interviews and a tracker to see how many have responded and how many more are needed.

**Respondent 7**

**Q1:** Mostly email. Sometimes through social media, with links sent through group chats, private messages, or other respondents.

**Q2:** Once. I was qualified as a respondent but refused to take part because the research was on a topic that was sensitive to me.

**Q3:** When inconsistencies start to pile up; for instance, the data quality lacks several values as given by a respondent. Response and sampling bias come into play when gathering data.

**Q4:** I think the project is a good idea, but it could become flawed with uncontrolled networking. A project that involves research studies and a number of people requires management and review.

**Respondent 8**

**Q1:** School email.

**Q2:** None.

**Q3:** Finding respondents who actually meet all our qualifications.

**Q4:** It’s good to have a system that will somewhat automate searching for respondents instead of doing it manually.

**Respondent 9**

**Q1:** Classroom visitations wherein researchers hand us physical questionnaires, but most of the time through digital means like email or QR codes.

**Q2:** For classroom visitations, I can’t really ignore or refuse. But for emails, I get lazy about reading what their research is about, let alone answering them.

**Q3:** Making the tools, asking for their validation, and looking for respondents. In my past experience, our research was answered through physical questionnaires, and some respondents left out other questions, which made it difficult for us to discuss the gathered data.

**Q4:** You mentioned that there will be a part where users can input their qualifications. Then the system should allow them to answer only the research studies they are qualified to answer.

**Respondent 10**

**Q2:** None.

**Q3:** Finding qualified respondents.
